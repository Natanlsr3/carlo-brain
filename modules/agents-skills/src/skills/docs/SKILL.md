# Docs: documents that say what the user thinks

Aim: a document the user wants to read and send, that says what they think, not what you imagine. Form counts as much as substance: airy, illustrated when it helps, never a wall of notes.

## 1. Alignment (adapt to the task, never identical)

- **Automatic task or an already validated format** (call prep in the usual format, notes from a transcript, tidying a draft): no alignment needed, just do it.
- **A document with reasoning** (a synthesis, an analysis, an application, a plan, a team document): the thinking happens WITH the user, before writing. Propose the plan in at most five lines (for whom, goal, sections, what the document will not say) and wait for their agreement.
- **Whenever you are unsure what they think**: ask one to three short questions they can answer in one spoken sentence. One question beats a wrong page. Write their answers down; they feed the next document.
- If the request is vague, restate it in one sentence before starting.

## 2. Substance

- Write mainly what the user validated, and what comes from sources (vault, files, mail, transcripts) you re-read during this task.
- An idea of your own is allowed when it helps, but present it as a proposal, explained in one line, never as a fact or as the user's opinion.
- A name, a figure, a date, a quote: sourced, or marked [to verify].
- Verification: for a document that goes to someone else, check each fact against its source. For a short personal document, no heavy review.

## 3. Form

- **Shared or sent** documents: a clean shareable document in the tool the user uses (Google Docs through the connector, Notion, or a published page). Nothing goes on a shared drive without their approval for THAT document.
- **Personal or small-team** documents: a clear readable document. A raw markdown file in the vault is a working source, not the deliverable.
- Always: a title, a "30 seconds" box at the top, short sections, paragraphs of at most three lines, lists, bold only on the essential, tables and diagrams when they make it clearer.
- Call prep: mostly who the person is (background, company, topics) and a few question ideas, plus the basics of the field that help during the call; little of your own reflection.

## 4. Final review

Only when worth the cost: a document sent to a third party, a long one, an application. Re-read against the request and the agreed plan: everything covered, nothing invented, readable as is. For a small task, skip it.

## Memory

What you learn from the user's answers (how they think about a subject, a format they like or reject) goes into the assistant's memory as a feedback note, and you tell the user in one line.

## What you hand back

The link or path of the document, three lines on what it contains, the points marked [to verify], and the questions still open.
