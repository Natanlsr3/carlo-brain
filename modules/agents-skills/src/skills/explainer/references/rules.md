# Rules: explaining so that it is understood AND remembered

A summary of findings from the learning-science literature (multimedia learning, cognitive load, retrieval practice). Evidence levels: [M] meta-analysis, [E] controlled experiments, [X] expert opinion or theory. Effect sizes quoted in that literature are medians from laboratory studies: orders of magnitude, not guarantees.

## Guiding principle

Materials make people **understand**; only active recall and spacing make them **remember** [M]. So every lesson ends with recall questions, cards and a review plan.

## Content rules

1. **Summary first**: at most five lines at the top, each a complete statement. Detail after. [X]
2. **Vocabulary before process**: define the terms and parts before explaining how it works (pre-training). [E]
3. **One new concept per section.** Reduce extraneous load. [X]
4. **Remove the superfluous**: no mood anecdotes, no decorative illustrations, no emojis (coherence principle). [E]
5. **Signal the essential**: one key idea per section, one bolded term per block, an announced plan (signalling). [M]
6. **Words plus a picture** for each key concept. A diagram must make a relation explicit: if removing it loses no relation, remove it. [E]
7. **Labels inside the picture**, not in a separate legend (spatial contiguity). [M]
8. **Worked example then exercise** for a novice; go straight to the exercise for someone who knows the topic (expertise reversal). [M]
9. **Two contrasting examples** per concept when possible. [X]
10. **An analogy is a structured comparison that says where it breaks.** Otherwise it creates a misconception. [M]
11. **Refute the common misconception explicitly** ("people think ... in fact ..."). [E]
12. **Conversational tone**: "you", short sentences (personalisation). [E]
13. **Questions that make the reader reason**, not recognise: "why", "what happens if". [M]
14. **Every factual claim carries a citation** to a numbered passage of the source. Anything not in the sources goes under "Limits", marked hypothesis or extrapolation. (The most common failure of automatic study aids is extrapolation beyond the source.)
15. **Say what is uncertain or contested.** No forced happy ending, no moral.

## Form rules (written sheet)

16. Lines of 55 to 75 characters, body text of at least 17 px if rendered, line spacing 1.5, AA contrast. [X]
17. Fixed semantic colour if the sheet is rendered: accent for the key idea, soft red for the misconception, green for the correction, grey for sources. Never colour alone to carry meaning. [E]
18. Collapsible sections and one block at a time reduce overload (segmentation); useful for readers who lose focus easily. [M/X]
19. A recall question at the end of each section, answer hidden. [M]
20. A concept map with **verbs on the links**, not a radial cloud. Reading a concept map helps; a decorative mind map does not. [M]
21. Citations that point to the exact passage. [X]

## Audio and video (if you ever produce them)

22. Video: 2 to 6 minutes, one concept per video; voice plus diagram, only keywords on screen; what is said appears at the same moment; build the diagram step by step; a question with a pause every 2 to 4 minutes; no background music. [E]
23. Podcast: two hosts with different angles (a careful expert, a curious sceptic who is sometimes wrong); a 20 second opening that says why it matters; the plan announced; a three-point recap. Banned: hollow superlatives, forced enthusiasm, fake pauses, digressions. [E/X]

## Retention

24. **Retrieval practice**: closed-book questions beat re-reading. [M]
25. **Spacing**: review on day 1, 3, 7, 21. [M]
26. **Interleaving**: mix topics in a review session once the basics are solid. [E]
